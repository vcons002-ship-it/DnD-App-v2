# Dice physics reference

The simulation uses standard Earth gravity (9.80665 m/s^2), a 16 mm acrylic d6 reference, and acrylic density 1190 kg/m^3. Convex mesh volume determines mass: a full 16 mm cube is 4.87424 g. All character skins use this acrylic physical profile; the obsidian/glass appearance does not imply stone/glass mass.

Sources: [NIST standard gravity](https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors/nist-guide-si-appendix-b9), [Chessex 16 mm dice catalog](https://www.chessex.com/images/2019%20CAT06.pdf), [ACRYLITE block weight data](https://www.acrylite.co/resources/knowledge-base/article/what-is-the-weight-of-acrylite-r-block-acrylic?category=product-properties). Acrylic density is rounded from the published block weights and dimensions.

Cannon uses uniformly scaled length units and grams to improve numerical conditioning of small rigid bodies. Gravity and launch velocity are converted by the same length scale. Thus changing display scale does not change physical gravity, die dimensions, or density. The responsive tray grows with larger pools, keeping the physical reference die size fixed. Other polyhedra use the same circumscribed radius, with mass calculated from their own volume.

The launch is an input: 0.9-1.15 m/s horizontally, starting 2 cm beyond the die's radius outside the roller's edge and 4.5-5 cm above the floor. Dice are released 85 ms apart, with lanes spread across the available opening. The viewing player's character rolls enter from the bottom, including reactions; other rollers get a stable other edge. Results remain server-authoritative.

Cannon integrates at 480 Hz. The visual roll plays at 0.6x simulation speed to make the small dice readable; gravity, mass, and contact calculations are unchanged. Number flashes and flights run at normal UI speed, starting after all dice reach their final settled poses, then in result-box order at fixed 160 ms intervals so late-settling dice do not interrupt the reveal cadence. Ordinary Coulomb friction and restitution handle contacts, with floor friction 0.12 and restitution 0.7, with wall/die-to-die friction 0.18 and restitution 0.6. These values retain more rebound and travel than the previous 0.3 profile. Forward tumble starts at 18-32 radians/s. The engine's standard 0.01 linear/angular damping and ordinary per-body sleep are used. There is no custom rolling-resistance torque, low-impact bounce override, forced group sleep or late-roll braking.

Before playback, at most eight deterministic trajectory candidates are simulated. A candidate is rejected if a die escapes, fails to settle, or finishes supported above the floor by another die. This only chooses a readable animation; it does not reroll game results or move dice after they land. If no valid candidate is found, the existing result-summary fallback remains available. The entry containment wall is excluded until each die clears it, as before.

## Calibration boundary

There is no universal friction value for all dice and trays. These consistent contact coefficients are a conventional simulation profile, not measurements of the illustrated materials. Gravity and mass have physical references; tray contact properties have not been calibrated against a real tray. Natural throws may stop quickly, strike a wall, or miss the walls entirely; no minimum visible rolling time or forced wall hit is imposed.
